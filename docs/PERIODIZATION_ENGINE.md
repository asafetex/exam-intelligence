# Periodization Engine

## Purpose

Study allocation should change as the exam approaches. A fixed weekly subject schedule ignores coverage, validated mastery, retention and urgency.

## Canonical phases

### 1. Foundation
Goal: establish prerequisite models and broad first coverage. More theory/worked examples are acceptable where candidate evidence shows no usable model.

### 2. Build
Goal: expand syllabus coverage and rapidly connect theory to application/questions.

### 3. Consolidation
Goal: reduce weak-node variance, strengthen retrieval and improve delayed retention.

### 4. Specific Preparation
Goal: increase target-bank/role specificity, unseen validation and exam-like question mixes.

### 5. Peak / Final Stretch
Goal: prioritize high-value gaps, recurrent/core patterns, full simulations and scoring strategy while avoiding low-ROI expansion.

### 6. Taper
Goal: preserve retrieval/confidence/readiness, reduce unnecessary cognitive load and avoid destabilizing last-minute overlearning.

## Phase inputs

The engine should eventually infer phase from:
- `days_to_exam`;
- syllabus/tree coverage;
- validated mastery distribution;
- retention state;
- simulation readiness;
- available study time/workload;
- fatigue/recovery indicators when explicitly tracked.

Do not encode a universal “30 days = final stretch” rule.

## Output

Periodization changes intervention weights and allocation constraints; it does not override urgent evidence blindly. Example: a foundational gap in a high-weight prerequisite may still require concept study late in preparation.
