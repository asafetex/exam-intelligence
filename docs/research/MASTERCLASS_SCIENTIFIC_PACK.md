# Masterclass Scientific Pack — Canonical Audit Bridge

## Scope

A private canonical package was previously built from **6 Masterclass transcripts + 19 attached scientific artifacts**. Every scientific attachment was fully inspected and assigned source-integrity, evidence and prohibited-inference rules.

This repository does not reproduce copyrighted full texts. It preserves the project-relevant audit and operational consequences.

Source IDs below correspond to the private `E01…E19` corpus manifest.

---

## E01 — Berke (2018), *What does dopamine mean?*

Type: perspective/review.

Use:
- dopamine participates in learning, incentive/value, action vigor, effort allocation, timing and attention;
- reject `dopamine = pleasure` and `dopamine = study gasoline` as literal models;
- do not infer user dopamine or prescribe a study schedule from synaptic language.

Product effect: visible value/progress and effort design are legitimate behavioral targets; dopamine is explanatory context only.

---

## E02 — Cools (2019), *Chemistry of the Adaptive Mind: Lessons from Dopamine*

Type: major narrative review across human/animal/pharmacological/computational evidence.

Use:
- cognition involves stability/flexibility tradeoffs;
- dopaminergic effects are region-, task- and baseline-dependent;
- `more dopamine` is not uniformly better;
- stimulant effects can be heterogeneous.

Product effect: adaptive difficulty/context beats “maximize activation”; medication remains outside educational automation.

---

## E03 — cognitive-effort / dopamine synthesis material

Type: scientific explanatory/research material focused on dopamine and cognitive effort.

Use:
- effort has subjective cost;
- expected benefit can influence willingness to perform demanding cognitive work;
- proximal goals/benefit salience can help explain adherence.

Product effect: reduce useless friction and expose meaningful proximal progress; never infer neurotransmitter level.

---

## E04 — *Dopamine promotes cognitive effort by biasing benefits versus costs of cognitive work*

Type: human pharmacological/PET-related cognitive-effort evidence.

Use:
- willingness to choose cognitive effort depends partly on cost/benefit valuation;
- effects vary with baseline and manipulation.

Product effect: procrastination diagnosis should inspect task cost, clarity, expected benefit and uncertainty—not character or “low dopamine.”

## E06 — Friend et al. (2017), basal-ganglia dysfunction / inactivity in obesity

Use only as mechanistic/contextual evidence about movement/motivation circuitry in a specific animal/metabolic context.

Do not translate obesity/inactivity animal findings directly into concurseiro study prescriptions.

## E07 — Haber et al. (2000), striatonigrostriatal ascending spiral

Type: primate neuroanatomy.

Use:
- reward/motivation, cognition and motor domains interact through complex circuitry;
- reject simplistic single-pathway “motivation becomes habit” diagrams.

Product effect: contextual mechanism only; no direct study intervention derived from E07.

---

## E08 — Lally et al. (2010), *How Are Habits Formed?*

Type: longitudinal naturalistic human habit study.

Key audit:
- wide variation in estimated automaticity development;
- often-quoted ~66 days was a median within a subset with model fit;
- one lapse did not automatically destroy progress;
- behaviors were relatively simple and self-selected.

Product effect:
- stable cue + repeated initiation;
- no 21/30/66-day universal law;
- lapses trigger resumption, not symbolic restart;
- study remains deliberate even if initiation becomes easier.

---

## E09 — Massar et al. (2018), sleep deprivation and attentional effort

Use:
- sleep deprivation increases perceived/cognitive cost of attentional effort and affects performance/preferences;
- motivation does not fully restore rested cognitive state.

Product effect:
- readiness gate;
- recovery can outrank a heavy block;
- difficult work shifts to better alertness window when possible.

---

## E10 — Orban et al. (2018), inattentive behavior / working memory in boys with ADHD

Type: small contextual clinical/analog study.

Use:
- attention depends on task/context/cognitive load;
- externalizing steps and reducing simultaneous working-memory demand can be practical compensations.

Do not:
- diagnose ADHD;
- infer that interest rules it in/out;
- claim study technique cures a disorder.

Product effect: task decomposition/support is available as a low-risk intervention independent of diagnosis.

---

## E11 — Schultz reward-prediction-error explanatory source

Use:
- difference between expected and obtained outcomes is a useful learning/decision concept;
- dopamine is associated with reward-prediction error in many paradigms but does not explain all learning.

Product effect:

`attempt → result → discrepancy → explanation → adjustment`

is a useful pedagogical loop.

Do not turn every correct answer into a claimed dopamine spike.

---

## E12 — Di Liegro et al. (2019), *Physical Activity and Brain Health*

Type: broad narrative review, mixed human/animal evidence.

Use:
- regular physical activity supports general health and may support cognition;
- proposed mechanisms include many pathways, with fine-grained causal evidence often indirect/heterogeneous.

Product effect:
- physical activity can be a supportive context variable or candidate experiment;
- no universal “15–20 min before study to raise BDNF/dopamine” rule.

## E15 — Söder et al. (2020), d-amphetamine / effort-based decision / reinforcement learning

Type: pharmacological human evidence.

Use:
- pharmacological effects on effort/reinforcement are dose/task/person dependent.

Product effect: reinforces exclusion of medication dosing/prescribing from the Learning OS.

---

## E16 — Treadway et al. (2009), EEfRT / effort expenditure for rewards

Use:
- behavior reflects tradeoffs among effort cost, reward magnitude and probability;
- task provides objective effort-choice measurement in research contexts.

Product effect:
- model study avoidance via observable cost/benefit/task variables;
- do not diagnose mood disorder from study choices.

---

## E17 — Tricomi et al. (2009), human habit learning / posterior dorsolateral striatum

Use:
- repeated behavior can shift toward habit-related neural patterns under particular training conditions.

Product effect:
- stable repeated entry context is plausible;
- do not equate habit formation with learning mastery.

---

## E18 — Volkow et al. (2011), *Addiction: Beyond Dopamine Reward Circuitry*

Type: review/model largely based on substance-use disorder evidence.

Use:
- addiction involves conditioning, habits, salience, motivation, inhibitory control, stress, decision systems and more than dopamine alone.

Product effect:
- reduce competing cues;
- never describe disciplined studying as “positive addiction” or design coercive reward escalation.

---

## E19 — Roschel et al. (2021), creatine / brain health

Type: narrative review; cognition evidence heterogeneous, often small and context-dependent.

Use:
- helpful mainly as a guard against overconfident supplement claims.

Product effect:
- creatine remains outside core study algorithm;
- no dose/brand or sleep-substitution recommendation.

# Cross-source conclusions used by Exam Intelligence

## Strong operational conclusions

1. behavior should be measured directly rather than inferred from neurochemistry;
2. sleep/readiness can constrain cognitive work;
3. cognitive effort is sensitive to perceived cost/benefit and task context;
4. feedback/discrepancy is useful for adaptation;
5. stable cues reduce entry friction over repeated behavior;
6. habit timing is variable, not a fixed day count;
7. neuroscience mechanisms are distributed/contextual, not one-transmitter explanations;
8. clinical pharmacology/supplements are not the core route to better exam learning.

## How the Masterclass is preserved

The Masterclass remains valuable as a behavior-regulation framework:
- focus/exclusion;
- readiness;
- challenge-skill calibration;
- active attention;
- immediate useful feedback;
- proximal progress;
- progressive overload/variation;
- habit environment;
- frustration/adherence management.

The scientific audit changes explanations where necessary without discarding those operational tools.
